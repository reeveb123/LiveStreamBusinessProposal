Chapter 01

Business profile

A flexible production partner for brands, sellers, organizers and private clients.

Business foundation

Business description

The company provides end-to-end live streaming and multimedia production, including planning, equipment deployment, live operation, recording, post-production and delivery. Projects can be produced on location, in a controlled studio, or through a hybrid workflow.

Mission

To provide accessible, reliable and professionally executed streaming and multimedia production that helps businesses and event organizers communicate effectively with their audiences.

Vision

To become a trusted regional provider of live commerce, event streaming and digital video production services.

Professionalism

Preparation, communication and technical standards at every stage.

Reliability

Equipment checks, rehearsals, backup plans and documented procedures.

Transparency

Clear pricing, inclusions, exclusions, deadlines and revision limits.

Target market

Commerce

Online sellers, e-commerce brands, product launches and retail promotions.

Events

Grand openings, mini concerts, corporate events, community programs and celebrations.

Creative

Marketing agencies, event organizers, content creators and businesses needing editing support.

Initial coverage may focus on the business's home city or province and nearby municipalities. Nationwide assignments can be quoted based on travel, equipment logistics, crew and accommodation requirements.

Chapter 03

A repeatable production workflow

Every project follows a clear sequence, with defined client approvals and technical checkpoints.

STEP 01

Discover

Confirm goals, date, location, platform, audience, scope and budget.

STEP 02

Plan

Site assessment, crew, camera positions, connectivity, graphics and schedule.

STEP 03

Build & test

Install equipment, route signals, check audio, verify stream and rehearse.

STEP 04

Go live

Monitor cameras, audio, encoder, network, graphics and recording status.

STEP 05

Deliver

Verify files, edit agreed assets, transfer deliverables and close the project.

Pre-production checklist

  • Client brief and signed scope
  • Venue inspection or virtual technical assessment
  • Platform access and stream destination confirmation
  • Graphics and branding approval
  • Connectivity, power and contingency plan

Live production monitoring

  • Camera feeds and program composition
  • Audio levels and speech intelligibility
  • Encoding performance and internet stability
  • Graphics, recording and storage availability
  • Incident documentation and client communication

Chapter 09

Quality assurance & risk management

Reduce avoidable failures through preflight testing, redundancy where practical, clear client responsibilities and documented incident response.

Technical quality control

  • Camera focus, exposure and white balance
  • Speech clarity and audio clipping checks
  • Stream resolution, frame rate and encoding
  • Upload stability and backup connectivity
  • Graphics accuracy and brand consistency
  • Recording status, storage and file integrity

Risk and response

  • Internet failure: test and prepare backup options.
  • Equipment failure: carry critical spares and alternative paths.
  • Power loss: use properly sized protection and backup plans.
  • Data loss: verify and back up essential recordings.
  • Safety risks: secure stands, cables and power distribution.
  • Platform outage: communicate limitations and resume when possible.
Operational boundary: Backup internet or a UPS reduces certain risks but does not guarantee uninterrupted streaming. Venue conditions, third-party platforms and telecommunications providers may remain outside the production team's control.

Chapter 11

Terms & conditions

A commercial framework to adapt into a signed service agreement. Have the final contract reviewed by a qualified Philippine legal professional.

11.1 Booking and confirmation

A booking is confirmed when the client accepts the quotation or signs the agreement and pays the required reservation fee. The written scope must identify date, location, schedule, package, crew, deliverables and fees. An inquiry or tentative hold is not a confirmed booking unless expressly confirmed.

11.2 Reservation fee and payment schedule

Suggested default: 50% reservation fee on confirmation and 50% before final delivery. Larger projects may use staged payments. The contract must clearly define fee refundability, deductions, cancellation treatment and committed costs, subject to applicable law.

11.3 Quotations and scope of work

Each quotation will list services, equipment, crew, duration, deliverables, exclusions, payment schedule and validity. Suggested validity is 15 calendar days unless otherwise stated. Scope changes may require a revised quotation.

11.4 Overtime and extended production

Coverage begins and ends at the times specified in the quotation. Additional time may incur overtime charges at the agreed rate and billing increment, subject to crew availability and venue restrictions. Client or event delays do not automatically extend coverage at no cost.

11.5 Client responsibilities

The client supplies accurate event information, approved materials, platform permissions, venue access, safe working conditions, required permits and participant permissions. The client appoints an authorized decision-maker, pays on time and remains responsible for product claims, inventory, orders and fulfillment unless separately contracted.

11.6 Platform compliance

The client is responsible for complying with applicable platform rules and account requirements. The provider may refuse content reasonably believed to violate law, platform rules or the agreement. Views, sales, engagement, account approval and algorithmic reach are not guaranteed.

11.7 Internet and platform limitations

Quality depends on venue connectivity, network congestion, encoding, platform conditions and other factors. The provider will perform reasonable checks and troubleshooting but cannot guarantee zero interruption or third-party platform availability unless a specific service level is expressly agreed.

11.8 Equipment ownership and damage

Supplied equipment remains the property of the provider or rental company. The client must not move, dismantle or operate equipment without authorization. Damage claims must be documented and allocated according to the agreement, evidence and applicable law, excluding ordinary wear and tear and responsibility that belongs to the provider.

11.9 Cancellation and rescheduling

Cancellation or rescheduling must be notified in writing. The agreement should define deadlines, fee treatment, recoverable preparation expenses, third-party commitments and rescheduling availability. Any retained amount must comply with the signed agreement and applicable law.

11.10 Force majeure

Neither party is responsible to the extent extraordinary events beyond reasonable control prevent performance. The affected party should notify the other as soon as practicable. The parties should attempt to reschedule or agree an alternative, with work performed and unavoidable expenses handled under the contract and applicable law.

11.11 Deliverables and revisions

Deliverables and revision limits must be listed in the quotation. Suggested default: up to two reasonable revision rounds within the approved brief. New concepts, additional versions, major direction changes or replacement content may incur additional fees.

11.12 Copyright and usage rights

The agreement identifies ownership and permitted use of final deliverables, raw footage, graphics, templates, music, stock assets and project files. Unless otherwise agreed, a license to use paid final deliverables may be granted after full payment for the stated purpose. Pre-existing tools and templates remain the provider's property. Third-party assets remain subject to their licenses.

11.13 Confidentiality

Both parties take reasonable measures to protect confidential commercial information. Unpublished products, strategies, private recordings and credentials may not be disclosed except as authorized or legally required. Portfolio use of non-public material requires appropriate prior written permission.

11.14 Privacy and personal data

The parties handle personal data in accordance with applicable Philippine privacy law, including the Data Privacy Act of 2012 where applicable. The client is responsible for required notices, permissions or lawful basis for recording and publishing participants. The provider uses personal data only as needed for the contracted services and legal obligations.

11.15 Limitation of liability

Any liability limitation must be clearly stated and comply with applicable law. The provider is not responsible for losses caused solely by third-party platform outages, venue failures, telecommunications disruptions or client systems beyond reasonable control. Liability that cannot lawfully be excluded remains unaffected.

11.16 Indemnification

Each party remains responsible for its own unlawful acts, contractual breaches and infringement of third-party rights. Any reimbursement obligation for third-party claims should define covered claims, notice and cooperation procedures, and remain subject to applicable law.

11.17 Termination

Either party may terminate for material breach that remains uncorrected after written notice and a reasonable opportunity to remedy, unless immediate termination is justified or required by law. The contract should define payment for completed work and approved or committed expenses.

11.18 Governing law and dispute resolution

The agreement is governed by applicable laws of the Republic of the Philippines. Parties should first attempt good-faith discussions and may pursue mediation or legal remedies if unresolved. Any venue or jurisdiction clause should be reviewed for enforceability.

11.19 Entire agreement

The signed service agreement, accepted quotation and approved statement of work form the agreement for the project. Amendments must be documented and accepted by authorized representatives of both parties.

Legal review note: This is a practical proposal template, not legal advice. Have cancellation, deposits, liability, privacy, intellectual property and tax terms reviewed before using them as a binding contract.
